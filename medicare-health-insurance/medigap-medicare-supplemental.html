Medigap (Medicare Supplemental)

Supplemental insurance (Medigap) policies work alongside your baseline coverage to absorb the remaining out-of-pocket hospital and doctor costs, keeping your healthcare expenses predictable.

Who it's for: Beneficiaries who want predictable medical bills and the freedom to see any doctor nationwide that accepts Medicare.

Why it matters: Medigap policies cover the "gaps" left by Original Medicare — such as copays, coinsurance, and deductibles — protecting you from high medical bills.

Planning timeline: Best secured during your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period starting the month you turn 65 and enroll in Part B.

Partner advantage: We compare lettered plans (Plan G, Plan N, etc.) across top-rated insurers to secure the lowest ongoing premium for identical coverage.

Medigap / Supplemental (Parts F, G, N, etc.)

The Basics

Medigap policies are extra insurance policies purchased from private companies to fill the financial "gaps" in Original Medicare — such as the 20% coinsurance, copays, and deductibles. Because Original Medicare doesn't cap your out-of-pocket spending, a Medigap plan provides a predictable financial safety net.

Medigap / Supplemental (Parts F, G, N, etc.)

The Details

Medigap policies are standardized secondary insurance policies purchased from private companies to absorb the out-of-pocket costs that Original Medicare leaves behind. Because Original Medicare does not pay for 100% of your medical bills and lacks a yearly spending limit, unexpected hospital stays or specialized treatments can result in high coinsurance and copayments. Medigap plans (designated by letters like Plan G or Plan N) pay for these remaining gaps, including your Part A and Part B coinsurance or deductibles. The federal government standardizes the core benefits for each lettered plan, meaning Plan G offers identical medical coverage regardless of which private insurance company you purchase it from.

Medigap Plan F: Plan F is only available to individuals who were eligible for Medicare prior to January 1, 2020. Federal regulations phased out new sales of Plan F for anyone who became eligible for Medicare on or after January 1, 2020 — if you were eligible prior to that date, you are grandfathered in and can still maintain or purchase Plan F. Plan F is historically known as the most comprehensive Medicare Supplement plan available, covering 100% of all out-of-pocket gaps left behind by Original Medicare, including your Part A hospital deductible, hospital coinsurance, skilled nursing facility coinsurance, Part B coinsurance, and — uniquely — even your annual Medicare Part B deductible. With Plan F, you pay your monthly premium and owe $0 out-of-pocket for any Medicare-approved services.

Medigap Plan G: Currently the most comprehensive, widely chosen Medicare Supplement plan available to new enrollees. It covers virtually all out-of-pocket gaps left behind by Original Medicare — including your Part A hospital deductible, hospital coinsurance, skilled nursing facility coinsurance, and Part B excess charges. The only out-of-pocket cost you pay with standard Plan G is the annual Medicare Part B deductible; once that small, once-a-year deductible is met, Plan G covers 100% of your remaining Medicare-approved medical costs. Because Medigap plans are standardized by the federal government, a Plan G policy provides identical medical coverage no matter which private insurance company you purchase it from.

Medigap Plan N: A popular, budget-friendly alternative for those who want strong financial protection against major medical bills while enjoying lower monthly premiums than Plan G. Plan N covers nearly all major Medicare gaps — including your Part A hospital deductible and your 20% Part B coinsurance. In exchange for a lower monthly premium, Plan N introduces modest cost-sharing: you are responsible for the annual Part B deductible, small office visit copays of up to $20, and emergency room copays of up to $50 that do not result in an inpatient hospital admission. Plan N does not cover Part B excess charges (extra amounts some doctors are legally allowed to charge above the Medicare-approved amount, though these are increasingly rare).
