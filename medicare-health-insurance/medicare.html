Medicare (Parts A & B)

The Basics

When you turn 65 or approach retirement, the government-backed healthcare system known as Original Medicare becomes your foundational safety net. It is split into two primary parts (A&B).

Medicare (Parts A & B)

The Details

Original Medicare is the federal government's healthcare program for people turning 65 or qualifying due to specific disabilities. It is divided into two primary parts that form your foundational medical safety net. Part A functions as your hospital insurance, helping cover inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facility care, hospice, and certain home health services. Most individuals receive Part A premium-free if they or their spouse paid Medicare payroll taxes for at least 10 years. Part B acts as your medical insurance, covering doctor visits, outpatient care, durable medical equipment, and preventive clinical screenings. Unlike Part A, Part B requires a standard monthly premium that can scale based on your individual income level. Together, Original Medicare covers roughly 80% of approved medical expenses, leaving the remaining 20% coinsurance uncovered without an annual out-of-pocket spending cap.

Part A (Hospital Insurance): Helps cover inpatient care in hospitals, skilled nursing facility care, hospice care, and some home health care. Most people get Part A automatically without paying a monthly premium if they or their spouse paid Medicare taxes while working.

Part B (Medical Insurance): Helps cover doctor visits, outpatient care, medical supplies, and preventive services. Unlike Part A, Part B requires a monthly premium, which is typically adjusted based on your income.

Important Note: Original Medicare covers about 80% of your approved medical costs. It does not cover everything (such as prescription drugs, routine dental, or vision), and it has no annual limit on your out-of-pocket spending. That is why building a complete plan around Parts A and B is essential.
