Understanding Guaranteed Income Options

Annuities and other guaranteed-income products aren't one-size-fits-all. Here's how to know if one belongs in your plan.

For decades, many retirees could count on a traditional pension to provide steady income throughout retirement. Today, fewer employers offer pensions, which means more of the responsibility for creating reliable income falls on personal savings, Social Security, and — for some retirees — guaranteed income products like annuities.

What "guaranteed income" means

Guaranteed income products, most commonly annuities, are designed to convert a portion of your savings into a predictable income stream, often for the rest of your life. The appeal is straightforward: rather than relying entirely on investment performance or manually managing withdrawals, a portion of your retirement income is set on a more predictable footing.

Why some retirees consider it

People who are especially concerned about outliving their savings, uneasy about relying on investment markets for essential expenses, or looking to replace the security a pension used to provide often find guaranteed income options worth exploring. It's rarely an all-or-nothing decision — many retirees use guaranteed income to cover essential expenses while keeping other savings invested for growth or flexibility.

Questions worth asking before deciding

What portion of your essential monthly expenses are already covered by Social Security or a pension? How would you feel if the market dropped significantly in the years right after you retire? Do you value predictability enough to trade some liquidity or growth potential for it? These are the kinds of questions that shape whether — and how much — guaranteed income makes sense for your specific plan.

How we help

We look at your full income picture — Social Security, pensions, and savings — before recommending anything, so any guaranteed income strategy fits alongside what you already have rather than duplicating or replacing it unnecessarily.
