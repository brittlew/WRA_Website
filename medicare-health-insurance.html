Medicare & Health Insurance

Medical insurance includes a wide range of coverage options, from bundled plans like Medicare Advantage to individually selected policies. We help both 65+ retirees and those under 65 find the right combination of coverage, even when spouses have different needs.

Medicare

Navigating original government healthcare sets the operational foundation for your retirement medical security, ensuring you access fundamental hospital and medical insurance protections without costly missteps.

Who it's for: Individuals turning 65 or transitioning out of employer coverage who need foundational government-backed healthcare.

Why it matters: Establishing your baseline Medicare Part A and Part B correctly sets the stage for all secondary health protections and avoids lifetime late-enrollment penalties.

Planning timeline: Begin exploring your options 3 months before your 65th birth month.

Partner advantage: We clarify Part A and B guidelines so you don't miss enrollment windows or face unexpected coverage gaps.

Medicare Advantage

These all-in-one private alternative plans combine your hospital, medical, and often prescription benefits into a single managed program that frequently bundles extra wellness perks.

Who it's for: Retirees looking for an "all-in-one" alternative to Original Medicare that often includes extra perks like dental and vision.

Why it matters: These bundled private plans manage your out-of-pocket maximums and streamline medical care into a single managed network.

Planning timeline: Evaluate or switch plans annually during the Medicare Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7).

Partner advantage: We cross-examine local network availability and plan benefits across carriers to find a fit for your specific doctors in Oregon, Washington, or California.

Medigap (Medicare Supplemental)

Supplemental insurance (Medigap) policies work alongside your baseline coverage to absorb the remaining out-of-pocket hospital and doctor costs, keeping your healthcare expenses predictable.

Who it's for: Beneficiaries who want predictable medical bills and the freedom to see any doctor nationwide that accepts Medicare.

Why it matters: Medigap policies cover the "gaps" left by Original Medicare — such as copays, coinsurance, and deductibles — protecting you from high medical bills.

Planning timeline: Best secured during your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period starting the month you turn 65 and enroll in Part B.

Partner advantage: We compare lettered plans (Plan G, Plan N, etc.) across top-rated insurers to secure the lowest ongoing premium for identical coverage.

Private Health Insurance (Under 65)

Individual health coverage bridges the gap for early retirees, entrepreneurs, and spouses who require compliant private medical insurance before reaching official Medicare age.

Who it's for: Self-employed individuals, early retirees, entrepreneurs, or spouses who haven't reached age 65 yet.

Why it matters: Finding an individual health plan prevents major financial vulnerability during the bridge years before Medicare eligibility begins.

Planning timeline: Review during annual open enrollment periods or when experiencing a qualifying life event (like job loss or relocation).

Partner advantage: We navigate individual and family health insurance exchanges to find cost-effective private plans tailored to your medical needs.

Prescription Drug Plan (PDP)

Standalone prescription drug plans protect your budget from escalating pharmaceutical tier costs by matching your precise medication regimen to the most cost-effective formulary.

Who it's for: Anyone on Medicare taking routine medications who wants to minimize pharmacy costs.

Why it matters: Drug formularies change yearly, and the wrong plan can lead to high out-of-pocket tier costs for essential prescriptions.

Planning timeline: Re-evaluate your prescription needs every fall during the Medicare Open Enrollment window.

Partner advantage: We run custom prescription cost analyses matching your exact medication list against local pharmacy formularies.

Vision

Standalone or integrated vision policies ensure you maintain regular eye health evaluations while managing the costs of prescription eyewear and specialized lenses.

Who it's for: Retirees and individuals wanting regular eye exams, glasses, or contact lens coverage outside of standard medical care.

Why it matters: Routine vision care ensures early detection of age-related eye conditions while offsetting the rising costs of lenses and frames.

Planning timeline: Integrated easily alongside your health or Medicare selections during annual enrollment.

Partner advantage: We bundle standalone vision options that complement your primary medical or Medicare Advantage selection.

Dental

Independent dental plans fill the coverage void left by standard Medicare, paying for essential preventative care and major restorative procedures like crowns or bridges.

Who it's for: Anyone looking to protect their oral health from expensive out-of-pocket restorative or preventative dental procedures.

Why it matters: Standard Medicare does not cover routine dental work (cleanings, fillings, crowns, dentures), making independent dental plans vital for fixed incomes.

Planning timeline: Add or review coverage alongside your health policy transitions.

Partner advantage: We source comprehensive dental policies featuring robust coverage for major services without restrictive waiting periods where possible.
